# ADR-00260: `.throw()` / `.return()` on async generators (unified generator swap)

- **Relations**: extends [ADR-00259](ADR-00259.md) (sync generator protocol) to async generators; the async-generator scope cut TDD-00086 named as a follow-on. Builds on [ADR-00253](ADR-00253.md) (async generators), [ADR-00244](ADR-00244.md)
- **Date**: 2026-08-20

## Context

[ADR-00259](ADR-00259.md) completed the sync-generator protocol (`.throw()`,
`.return()`, `yield*`) on the back of two mechanisms: **resume-mode dispatch** at
the `yield` point and a **per-generator jmpbuf stack + outer body catch-all** for
sound exception isolation. Async generators ([ADR-00253](ADR-00253.md)) shared the
`yield` path (so resume-mode dispatch already applied to them) but kept their own
per-`.next()` `setjmp` guard for throw→reject and had no `.throw()`/`.return()` —
TDD-00086 explicitly deferred them.

The per-`.next()` setjmp had the same cross-fiber jmpbuf-inversion weakness the
sync side just fixed: an injected `.throw()` into a body with an internal `try`
would land on the caller's frame, not the body's. So the honest way to add async
`.throw()`/`.return()` is to put async generators on the *same* isolation the sync
side now uses.

## Decision

Unify the swap. The body-side machinery is already shared (the `yield` resume-mode
dispatch, the outer catch-all — now emitted for async bodies too, not just sync).
The caller side factors into one core:

- **`emitGeneratorSwapCore(genObj, genTy, resetCurrentTask)`** does the isolated
  swap — activate the generator's own jmpbuf stack, swapcontext, restore the
  caller's — with an `resetCurrentTask` flag that adds the async generator's
  `__kml_current_task` save/null/restore (so body `await`s busy-drive rather than
  mis-park the consumer, unchanged from [ADR-00253](ADR-00253.md)).
- **Sync** resumers wrap it with a `__genError` **re-throw** on the caller side.
- **Async** resumers wrap it with `emitAsyncGenSwapAndSettle`: after the swap,
  `__genError` non-null ⟹ **reject** the `.next()`/`.throw()`/`.return()` promise;
  else settle it fulfilled with `{value, done}`.

The former per-`.next()` setjmp guard is gone — an uncaught body throw (including
one an injected `.throw()` triggers, or a failed `await` in the body) now lands in
the outer catch-all on the generator's own jmpbuf stack, which records `__genError`
and swaps back; the async resumer rejects from there.

`.throw(e)` / `.return(v)` mirror the sync by-value helpers, settling a promise
instead of returning a raw result: a suspended generator resumes in mode 1 / mode
2 through the shared core; a not-started or done generator rejects with `e`
(throw) or fulfils `{value: v, done: true}` (return) without running the body.
`emitGeneratorThrowByValue`/`emitGeneratorReturnByValue` dispatch to the async
variants on `GeneratorIsAsync`, so the call-site wiring (emit_call.go) and result
typing (`Promise<{value,done}>`) are already in place from ADR-00259/00253.

## Consequences

- `.throw(e)` on an async generator: a body `try/catch` (which may `await`)
  handles it and the promise fulfils; otherwise the promise rejects and the
  generator finishes. `.return(v)`: runs enclosing `finally`s and fulfils
  `{value: v, done: true}`; the not-started/done cases settle without running the
  body.
- Async generators now share the exact isolation the sync side uses, so a throw
  inside an async generator body is robust against caller-interleaving `try`s —
  the same property the sync unification bought.
- Async `yield*` is still out of scope (sync generators only, ADR-00259).
