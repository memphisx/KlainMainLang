# TDD-00183: Windows IOCP reactor (completion-model I/O)

- **Status**: Not Started
- **Date**: 2026-09-07

## Context

[TDD-00180](TDD-00180.md) identified two ways out of the Windows CRT-fd-table debt, and [TDD-00182](TDD-00182.md) designs and sequences the first — **Option A**, an owned handle table that keeps the readiness/`select()` shape — because it is the smaller change and touches no IR-level networking code. This document records the second, **Option B, an IOCP reactor**, as a committed follow-on goal.

B is **lower priority than A but is not deferred.** A removes the CRT from the Windows I/O path; B is the step that makes that I/O *natively* scalable and is the faithful long-term reactor — the completion model Windows is built around and the one libuv uses on Windows. The project's near-term targets (CLI tools, Docker microservices) do not yet demand IOCP's connection scale, which is why A comes first; but "readiness emulated over a bounded wait" remains a ceiling, not an endpoint, and B is the intended destination. Scoping B now — rather than after A ships — keeps it a first-class tracked goal and, more concretely, lets A be **built so B reuses its foundation** instead of discarding it (this is [TDD-00182](TDD-00182.md)'s Open question 5, answered here as a design constraint on A: **yes, shape A's table and wakeup for B**).

## Design

The essential difference from A is the I/O model (see [TDD-00182](TDD-00182.md)'s Design for the readiness/completion contrast):

- **Sockets** move from readiness (`select`/`WSAEventSelect`) to **completion**: overlapped `WSARecv`/`WSASend`/`AcceptEx`/`ConnectEx`, each socket associated with a single I/O completion port; the event loop becomes one `GetQueuedCompletionStatusEx` drain. Named pipes and `fs.watch` become natural under this model (they are overlapped by construction).
- **The IR-level callers** — `runtime_net.go`, `runtime_http.go`, `runtime_stdin.go`, and the TLS paths — are exactly the surface A was chosen to preserve, and are what B has to touch. Their "wait for readiness, then issue the read/write" shape becomes "post the read/write up front, handle the completion." Two sub-approaches trade churn against faithfulness:
  - **(i) A proactor→reactor shim** that presents the existing `select()`-shaped contract over an IOCP underneath (the loop posts reads internally and reports "readable" when a completion lands). Minimal IR churn; costs an extra buffer copy and some fidelity at the edges.
  - **(ii) A genuine completion API** the IR adopts directly. Most faithful and the natural home for zero-copy paths; the largest change, rewriting the callers A protected.
- **What A already provides that B reuses**, so B is an evolution of A rather than a rewrite: the owned, kind-tagged handle table with refcounted descriptions; non-CRT handle ownership; and the single process-wide wakeup object (under IOCP, a custom completion packet posted with `PostQueuedCompletionStatus` plays the wakeup role). B replaces A's `WaitForMultipleObjectsEx` wait with the completion port; the table, handle lifetimes, and kind dispatch carry over unchanged.

A recommended direction between (i) and (ii) is deliberately left open until A is built and its handle table's real shape is known — that is the point of sequencing B after A rather than designing both in full now.

## Open questions

1. **Shim vs native completion API** (sub-approach i vs ii): the churn-versus-faithfulness call, decided once A's handle table exists.
2. **TLS over IOCP**: how overlapped socket I/O composes with the current TLS transport (libcurl / schannel) without a full rewrite of the TLS paths.
3. **Retire or retain A's wait**: does the IOCP port subsume all handles, or does A's `WaitForMultipleObjects` path stay for non-socket handles that gain nothing from IOCP?
4. **Sequencing**: immediately after A ships, or after the incremental §2–§6 Windows pile fully clears — B is committed either way, this is only *when*.
