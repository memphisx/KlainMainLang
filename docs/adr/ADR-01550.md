# ADR-01550: Promise members of a union

- **Relations**: Extends TDD-00114, TDD-00230
- **Date**: 2026-10-10

## Context

- A mixed array literal with a promise element (`[Promise.reject(e),
  "u"]`) was inferred as `any[]`, where TypeScript infers
  `(Promise<never> | string)[]` and an annotation of that type already
  held the union.
- In a union with a Promise member, `typeof z === "string" … else
  z.then(…)` failed to compile ("a number has no method 'then'"): a
  promise member had no `typeof` tag, so the complement picked the
  wrong member.
- A union box narrowed to its promise member was unwrapped as a raw
  pointer, not through the Promise<any> wrapper emitBoxPromise puts it
  behind: `.then` on it threw at run time.

## Decision

- A mixed literal's union may hold promise members (one per distinct
  promise representation).
- A promise member's `typeof` tag is "object".
- Unboxing a box to a Promise type unwraps the Promise<any> and
  converts it to the target's element type, as the coercion from `any`
  does (a `never` element needs no conversion); `x as Promise<T>` over a
  union box unboxes the same way.
- Promise.all/allSettled/race/any over an array of a union with promise
  members await each member (Awaited<T> over the union): the result's
  element is the union of the awaited members.
- A mixed literal's union drops a promise of `never` (`Promise.reject(…)`)
  when another promise member is present, as TypeScript's subtype
  reduction does; `allSettled` over `[Promise.resolve(1),
  Promise.reject("x")]` keeps its per-status result shape.
- The checker-side mapping of a union read narrows as emission does: to
  its promise member, or to the members a typeof test leaves, in
  declared order.
